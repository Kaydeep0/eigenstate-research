# Tether's Audit Is Public. The Custody Fee Path Beside It Is Still Open.

*Money in Motion · Eigenstate Research · September 02, 2026*

Tether's first full KPMG audit is on the front page. Coverage reads the reserves. The open question beside that audit is who gets paid to hold and roll the Treasuries behind USDT, and how clearly that path shows up next to Circle's.

Hold one object in your head: custody. Audit language answers whether the reserves look clean. Custody fees answer who gets paid to keep and roll them.

Circle spreads asset management, custody, and primary-dealer roles across BlackRock, BNY Mellon, and a separate dealer. Tether concentrates more of that path at Cantor Fitzgerald (press-cited custody; weaker than Circle to BNY on EDGAR).

In other words: the audit and the fee path are different layers on the same stack. Against Circle's named path, the thinner custody disclosure is the gap.

Circle's reserve stack is the dated contrast: named manager, named custodian, and EDGAR-visible distributor economics on USDC. Tether's custody economics stay thinner in public filings beside an audit that is now front-page.

I'd treat this reading as wrong if the next disclosures put Tether's custody fees on EDGAR with the same clarity as Circle-to-BNY, or show Cantor's role was never a paid custody path.

The key point is simple. Audit language and custody fees are different layers. Against Circle's named path, the custody fee layer is what the audit headline leaves underspecified.
